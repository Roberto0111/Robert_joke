## Five-part structural analysis — fallback, not observed source analysis

1. Recognition hook: an immediately visible contradiction, lighting a scented gift while pinching one's nose; the cat names the action before any abstract explanation.
2. Concrete scene: the same scent arrives again in a gift box beside the already-used jar. Thoughtful repetition is visible before the explanation.
3. Escalation / assumption break: the cat asks whether the friend has ever been told the actual preference. This redirects attention from the gift toward the feedback.
4. Emotional turn: Roberto admits that fear of disappointing the friend led him to repeat praise. It is his strategy, not a diagnosis or a verdict on the relationship.
5. Final compression: the friend remembers accurately, but remembers the invented preference. A recipient might privately send this to a friend who keeps praising gifts they do not enjoy; this is only a sharing hypothesis.
